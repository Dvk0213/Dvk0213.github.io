Hello!

I am Shengqi, a senior undergraduate in Mathematics and Computer Science at University of Wisconsin-Madison (UW-Madison) advised by Prof. Frederic Sala and Prof. Benedek Valkó.

I am applying to Ph.D. programs for Fall 2027.

My research interests are in Data-Centric AI, Foundation Models, AI Agents, and Mathematics. In particular, I work on:

  • Hybrid Program–LLM Evaluation: Developed $\textbf{\textsc{Pajama}}$, a hybrid evaluation framework that distills LLM judging logic into executable Python programs, aggregates their predictions via weak supervision, and selectively routes low-confidence cases to LLM judges using probabilistic confidence estimates. The system advances the accuracy–throughput Pareto frontier and enables competitive reward-model training with 45–50× lower labeling cost than GPT-4-based supervision.
  • Hybrid Transformer–SSM Architectures: Investigated how layer ordering affects the expressivity of hybrid Transformer–SSM(TF-SSM) models under resource constraints. Designed two novel tasks, Segmented Retrieval and Exact Copy, that reveal the advantages of TF-first over SSM-first architectures. Established recurrent-state memory lower bounds for SSM-first models, developed efficient TF-first constructions, and extended the theoretical results to convolutional SSMs, including Mamba. Empirically validated the theoretical predictions, with TF-first hybrids achieving 100% accuracy on Segmented Retrieval, compared to below 50% for the competing two-layer architectures.
  • Random Matrix Identities: Investigated the two-point correlation function of the $\mathbf{Sine}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}$ process through ODEs and symbolic computation. For $ \beta=6$, independently discovered an operator factorization that reduces a third-order ODE to solvable lower-order equations, enabling a new explicit Bessel-function representation of the pair correlation function. We further derived its large-$\lambda$ asymptotic expansion and established agreement with Forrester's earlier results.
